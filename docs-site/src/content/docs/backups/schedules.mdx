TenuVault can back up each tenant automatically, daily or weekly, at a time you choose. Scheduled backups run inside the TenuVault app on your computer, as you, with no cloud service involved. This page explains how to set up a schedule and how to keep TenuVault running so the schedule is met.

## How scheduled backups run

* Each tenant has its own schedule: daily or weekly, at a time in your computer's local timezone. Daylight saving and timezone changes are followed. A time skipped by the spring clock change moves forward by the clock change. A repeated autumn time uses its first occurrence, with one attempt budget for both occurrences.
* Backups run while TenuVault is running, in the window or in the system tray (the menu bar on macOS) after you close the window.
* TenuVault checks the schedules every minute. The first check runs 30 seconds after TenuVault starts.
* If the computer was off or TenuVault was not running at the scheduled time, the missed backup runs once as soon as TenuVault starts again. Only the most recent missed slot runs, not every missed one.
* When several backups are due, they run one after another.
* A failed scheduled backup is retried after 15 minutes, with up to three attempts in the same slot. Each attempt belongs to the slot when it started. After the third failure, TenuVault waits until the next scheduled time. If that time passed during the failed run, it is due at the next check with a fresh attempt budget. A successful backup resets the attempt count.
* Saving a schedule never triggers a catch-up backup for slots before the change.
* Each scheduled backup uses the tenant's saved backup choice. See [Choose what automatic backups cover](#choose-what-automatic-backups-cover).

## Set up a schedule

1. Select the tenant in the tenant switcher.
2. Open **Backup & Restore** and click the **Schedule** tab. You can also click **Set up automatic backups** or **Edit schedule** on the Dashboard.
3. Under **Automatic backups**, tick **Back up** *tenant name* **automatically**.
4. In **Repeat**, choose **Every day** or **Every week**.
5. For a weekly backup, choose the day in **Day**.
6. In **Time**, choose the time. The label shows your computer's timezone.
7. Click **Save**.

TenuVault confirms with **Schedule saved**. The new schedule starts at 02:00 by default, and a weekly schedule starts on Sunday.

If the time is empty or invalid, **Choose a time, for example 07:45.** appears beside **Time**. Selecting **Save** moves focus to that field without saving. Your recurrence and weekday stay selected.

To pause automatic backups on any plan, clear the check box and click **Save**. TenuVault confirms with **Automatic backups paused**. The saved recurrence, time, weekday and last result are kept. You can pause an existing daily schedule after moving from Pro or MSP to Community. Its recurrence summary stays visible while it is paused. Enabling daily backups again requires Pro or MSP.

:::note
Daily backups are part of Pro and MSP. Before the tenant's plan information has loaded, **Checking plan** appears beside **Repeat**. If the license check has returned but no plan is available for the tenant, **Plan not verified** appears with the license coverage warning. Daily backups are unavailable in both cases. On Community, **Repeat** offers **Every day (Pro)** as an unavailable option and you schedule weekly backups.
:::

If a schedule is saved and the background runner is not installed, the summary shows: **Background restart is not installed. Backups run while TenuVault is running.** Under **Background runner**, use **Settings > Background runner** to open Settings, then select **Background runner** to manage automatic restarts.

On Community, beneath the daily schedule note, the editor explains that a weekly schedule can leave up to six days of changes unprotected; daily scheduling is included in TenuVault Pro and MSP.

### The Status panel

Next to the schedule, **Status** shows:

| Field | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Next backup** | When the next automatic backup runs, or **Not scheduled** |
| **Last automatic backup** | When the last successful attempt ran and its result message. After a failure, the label changes to **Last automatic backup attempt failed** and shows the failed attempt's time and message. |
| **Kept for** | How long backups are kept. See [Retention](/backups/storage/#retention). |
| **Not backed up because of the license** | Recent automatic backups TenuVault refused to run, with the time, whether they were scheduled or started from the menu bar or tray icon, and the reason |

## Choose what automatic backups cover

Below the schedule, **What to back up** holds the tenant's saved backup choice: **Everything except apps**, **Everything** or **Custom**. It is "Used by automatic backups and "Back up all tenants now", and preselected when you run a backup by hand."

1. Choose the option, or with **Custom** tick the areas and types to include.
2. Click **Save**.

A tenant that never saved a choice uses **Everything except apps**, shown as "(default)". See [What gets backed up](/backups/coverage/) for the types in each area.

## License and plan checks

Before every automatic backup, TenuVault checks that:

* your license covers the tenant
* the plan includes daily backups, for a daily schedule
* the plan includes Azure storage, when the tenant's backups go to an Azure storage account

If a check fails, the backup does not run. TenuVault shows a notification, records the refusal under **Not backed up because of the license** and shows "Not backed up:" with the reason as the schedule's last result. When the tenant is not licensed, the Schedule tab also says "Your license does not cover" the tenant "right now, so its automatic backups do not run."

Refusals are kept on this device, not in the tenant's audit log, because the tenant's storage is not opened without a license. TenuVault keeps the 200 most recent refusals across all tenants.

## When a scheduled backup fails

* TenuVault shows a notification: "Scheduled backup of *tenant* failed", with the reason. A backup started from the tray uses "Tray backup of *tenant* failed". Click either notification to bring TenuVault to the front and open that tenant's **Schedule** tab, even if its window was closed.
* If a tray request and a scheduled request overlap for one tenant, they share the running backup. A failure produces one notification, named for the request that started the backup.
* The notification uses the tenant's friendly name, then its domain, then "this tenant" if neither is available. It does not use the tenant ID as the label. An error message can still contain an ID supplied by the service that reported it.
* The Schedule tab, the Dashboard and the All tenants page show **Last automatic backup attempt failed**, with the failed attempt's time. The Schedule tab also shows its result message.
* On All tenants, the full failure timestamp wraps and **Action required** appears. Click the failure row to open that tenant's schedule.
* Dashboard successful and failed counts use stored backup history. If the last automatic attempt failed and no backup is stored, the status says "Automatic backup failed before a backup was saved." The **Failed** count can remain zero.
* The result is recorded in the tenant's [audit log](/audit-log/) as "Scheduled backup".
* **Next backup** shows the retry time. Retries wait 15 minutes after a failed run, with a maximum of three attempts per slot, including the first run. After three failures, the next attempt is at the next scheduled time. Retry state is saved when TenuVault closes.

Requests that stop responding time out after 90 seconds, including encrypted Azure uploads. TenuVault retries temporary connection and service failures before reporting a policy warning or a failed backup. If an upload loses its response and a retry is refused, it counts as saved only when the stored content matches the intended backup. Graph throttling pauses all readers for the server's requested interval, up to five minutes. A longer interval fails the request so the backup can finish and be retried later. Once the run finishes, later backups can start again.

When Microsoft Graph or backup storage stops responding during a backup, the backup stops within a few minutes and is retried under the schedule rules above. Successful requests to one service do not reset repeated failures from the other. If removing old backups fails, cleanup stops and the saved backup remains completed with a warning. Old backups that were not removed are reconsidered on the next run.

If your sign-in has expired or Conditional Access requires you to sign in again, the backup fails and a banner at the top of the TenuVault window asks you to **Sign in**. Scheduled backups never open a sign-in window by themselves.

## Keep TenuVault running

Scheduled backups only run while TenuVault runs. Three settings help with that. They apply to all tenants and are saved right away.

In **Settings** > **Background runner**, these labels say **menu bar** on macOS and **tray** on Windows. The **Schedule** tab uses **tray** on both platforms.

### Keep running in the menu bar or tray

**Keep running in the menu bar/tray when I close the window** is on by default. Closing the window then hides it, and TenuVault keeps running in the system tray (Windows) or the menu bar (macOS). The first time, a notification says "TenuVault is still running".

When this setting is off, closing the window quits TenuVault on Windows.

### The menu bar or tray icon

Click the menu bar icon on macOS or the tray icon on Windows to open TenuVault. Its menu has:

| Item | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Open TenuVault** | Shows the window |
| **Back up all tenants now** | Backs up every connected tenant right away with its saved backup choice. The license and plan checks above apply. |
| **Next backup:** | The next scheduled backup across all tenants, or **No scheduled backups** |
| **Quit TenuVault** | Quits the current app process and stops scheduled backups. When background launch is installed, the operating system may reopen TenuVault within five minutes. To stop restarts completely, select **Settings** > **Background runner** > **Remove background launch** and turn off **Start TenuVault in the menu bar/tray when I sign in to this computer** before quitting. |

Backups started from the menu bar or tray icon are shown as **From tray** in the backup history.

### Start at login

**Start TenuVault in the menu bar/tray when I sign in to this computer** opens TenuVault in the menu bar on macOS or the tray on Windows, without a window, when you sign in to your computer. It is off by default.

You find both check boxes on the **Schedule** tab under **Background runner**, and in **Settings** under **Background runner**.

### Background launch

Background launch registers a task with your operating system that starts TenuVault in the menu bar on macOS or the tray on Windows when you sign in to the computer, and again every five minutes. If TenuVault is not running, it is reopened there within five minutes. Background launch is available in installed Windows and macOS builds.

1. Open **Settings** and go to **Background runner**.
2. Under **Restart TenuVault for scheduled backups**, click **Install background launch**.

<Tabs>
<TabItem label="Windows">

TenuVault creates a Task Scheduler task for your user account, named **TenuVault Background** followed by your account's security identifier. It runs at logon and every five minutes, with your normal (limited) rights. No password is stored.

</TabItem>

<TabItem label="macOS">

TenuVault installs a LaunchAgent for your user account that runs at login and every five minutes and opens TenuVault in the background. No password is stored.

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

If TenuVault is already running, the task does nothing: TenuVault runs only once.

Background launch has limits:

* It runs only while you are signed in to the computer. It does not run while you are signed out, or while the computer is asleep or off, and it does not wake the computer.
* Backups still need a valid Microsoft sign-in, a license that covers the tenant and available storage. It does not bypass MFA, Conditional Access, licensing or encryption.
* It does not guarantee a backup at the exact scheduled minute. The missed backup runs when TenuVault starts.
* If you move the TenuVault application, install background launch again.

To stop automatic restarts, open **Settings** > **Background runner**, click **Remove background launch** and turn off **Start TenuVault in the menu bar/tray when I sign in to this computer** before you quit. Removing background launch leaves your schedules unchanged and does not stop a backup that is running.
